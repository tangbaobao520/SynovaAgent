0. Executive Summary

Core Finding: Managerial Economics and Synova's existing theory base form a landscape of "7 complements, 3 gaps, 2 new frontiers."

Of the 7 Managerial Economics modules (Thomas 12th ed.):

  • ME4 (Information Economics & Agency) is largely MERGED with existing Shannon/Arrow/Galbraith information theory. The ME version provides a more business-oriented framing but does not add fundamentally new causal dimensions beyond what Synova's SIGNAL_TRANSMITS edge already models.
  • ME5 (Organization & Transaction Costs) forms natural BRIDGES with Coase/Williamson/Grossman-Hart. The existing ASSET_LOCKS edge already models asset specificity and governance. ME adds the four-type transaction cost taxonomy and the make-vs-buy decision framework.
  • ME1 (Demand Theory) and ME3 (Market Structure) COMPLEMENT the behavioral economics pillar (T7-T10). ME provides rational baselines; behavioral economics provides deviation diagnostics.
  • ME2 (Production & Cost Theory) and ME7 (Government Regulation) constitute clear GAPS -- no existing theory root covers them. ME2 in particular is the single largest capability gap.
  • ME6 (Capital Budgeting) has partial bridges (NPV with behavioral T7-T8-T10, Real Options with T14 Dynamic Capabilities) but the Real Options dimension is a NEW FRONTIER neither fully covers.
Reading guide: This document is structured for progressive reading. Start with the Executive Summary, then the Three-Layer Pyramid (Part II), then the Integration Proposals (Part VI). The matrix table (Part I) and detailed analyses (Parts III-V) serve as reference material.

1.4 Key Cell Justifications

ME1.1 Demand Elasticity x T7/T8 (Behavioral Economics) >>> BRIDGE

Precondition: Rational elasticity calculation (ME) requires ceteris paribus -- all non-price demand factors held constant. This precondition is rarely fully satisfied in real enterprise diagnostics (boundary condition).

Bridge logic: ME provides the "rational baseline" -- given price/income/cross elasticities, how should demand change? Prospect Theory (T7) and Mental Accounting (T8) explain why actual demand deviates from rational prediction. For example: a company cuts price by 10%. Rational prediction: ED=-2.0 implies demand +20%. Actual: only +8%. Diagnostic annotation: "Demand response deviates from rational baseline by -12pp. Possible causes: (1) loss aversion makes consumers less sensitive to price decreases than increases (T7), (2) mental accounting classifies this product as 'existing budget item' rather than 'new expenditure' (T8), (3) customer lock-in effect (LOCKS_IN) reduces demand elasticity." This dual-layer framework of "rational baseline + behavioral deviation" is the core value of ME x behavioral economics integration.

Academic reference: Kahneman & Tversky (1979) "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk." Econometrica 47(2): 263-291. Thaler (1980) "Toward a Positive Theory of Consumer Choice." Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization 1(1): 39-60.

ME2.2 Cost Curves / Break-Even Point x ALL 14 Theory Roots >>> PURE GAP

This is the single largest gap in the integration matrix. Break-even analysis (BEP = FC/(P-VC)), operating leverage (DOL = (Revenue-VC)/Profit), short-run cost curves (MC/ATC/AVC) -- these are core ME analytical tools with zero direct correspondence in any of the 14 existing theory roots. The closest is the CAPITAL_ALLOCATES edge's capital_efficiency parameter, but that focuses on capital returns rather than cost structure.

Impact: This is Synova's most obvious diagnostic blind spot. JTBD demands like "I need to know the true cost per unit" (U-JTBD-0013), "I don't know if a 5% price cut still leaves profit" (U-JTBD-0015), "I need to know per-SKU margin after discounts" (U-JTBD-0029) all depend on cost structure analysis -- and none of these are currently covered by any sentinel or compute function. The existing CUMULATIVE_LEARNING edge models dynamic efficiency changes (learning curve), but the static cost structure analysis capability is entirely missing.

Academic reference: Thomas & Maurice, Ch. 8-9 "Production and Cost in the Short Run / Long Run." The BEP formula and DOL concept are standard ME textbook material.

ME4.1 Information Asymmetry x T1/T2/T3 (Information Theory) >>> MERGE

Precondition: Both Shannon's mathematical channel capacity model and ME's information asymmetry model assume information can be quantified and transmitted. In real organizations, some information is tacit (Polanyi 1966) and resists quantification -- both frameworks have this shared boundary condition.

Merge logic: The existing Shannon/Arrow/Galbraith triad already models information degradation in organizations. ME's contribution (Akerlof's lemons market, Spence's signaling, Stiglitz's screening) provides the business application layer on top of the mathematical foundation. The key insight: SIGNAL_TRANSMITS edge already computes fidelity. What ME adds is that low fidelity triggers specific market failures (adverse selection in hiring, moral hazard in outsourcing, lemons problem in procurement). The merge is: SIGNAL_TRANSMITS computes the measurement, ME provides the diagnostic interpretation of what degraded fidelity means in specific business contexts.

Academic reference: Akerlof, G. (1970) "The Market for Lemons." Quarterly Journal of Economics 84(3): 488-500. Spence, M. (1973) "Job Market Signaling." Quarterly Journal of Economics 87(3): 355-374. Arrow, K. (1974) The Limits of Organization.

ME5.1 Transaction Costs x T4/T5/T6 (Transaction Cost Economics) >>> MERGE

Merge logic: The existing Coase-Williamson-Grossman-Hart triad is itself the foundation of ME's transaction cost module. Williamson's "asset specificity x frequency x uncertainty" framework is taught in both ME textbooks and organizational economics courses. The ME version adds the four-type transaction cost taxonomy (search/negotiation/enforcement/monitoring) as a pedagogical tool, but does not add fundamentally new causal dimensions beyond what the ASSET_LOCKS edge already models. Recommendation: upgrade the existing ASSET_LOCKS edge documentation to use ME's four-type taxonomy as the diagnostic narrative language, but do not add new compute functions.

ME6.2 Real Options x T14 (Dynamic Capabilities) >>> NEW FRONTIER

Precondition: Real options analysis requires estimating future volatility of cash flows, which in SME contexts (5-1000 people, Synova's core market) has very wide confidence intervals. The boundary condition is: real options is most applicable when there is sufficient historical volatility data (>3 years) and when the enterprise faces discrete, irreversible investment decisions with staged commitment options.

New frontier logic: ME teaches real options as "the value of flexibility in irreversible investment decisions under uncertainty." T14 (Dynamic Capabilities) teaches "sense-seize-transform" as the enterprise-level capacity to adapt. The intersection is: real options provides the micro decision framework for specific investments, while dynamic capabilities provides the macro organizational capacity that enables real options to be exercised. Neither framework currently exists as a computable entity in Synova -- this is a new frontier that would require: (1) a new parameter on the CAPITAL_ALLOCATES edge (optionality_value, already defined in v2.4 spec but unimplemented), (2) expanding the VOLATILITY_ARBITRAGES edge to include option exercise logic, and (3) potentially a new sentinel that detects "option value destruction" (when enterprises fail to recognize or exercise embedded real options).

Academic reference: Trigeorgis, L. (1996) Real Options: Managerial Flexibility and Strategy in Resource Allocation. MIT Press. Teece, D.J. et al. (1997) "Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management." Strategic Management Journal 18(7): 509-533.

Pyramid Value Proposition: Any diagnostic conclusion visible to the CEO has a complete reasoning chain — "Your signal attenuation has reached critical level" (Layer 3 output) → because Shannon entropy = X multiplied by Arrow hierarchy decay coefficient = Y (Layer 2 computation) → which in managerial economics terms means "each additional management layer reduces information fidelity by approximately 10%" (Layer 1 explanation). Preconditions: (1) Layer 1 microeconomic principles assume relatively stable market environments. In highly disruptive industries (e.g., AI-native companies), demand and cost function assumptions may break down rapidly. Diagnostic reports should annotate "This analysis is based on current market structure assumptions." (2) Layer 2 computable indicators assume sufficient data quality (missing value handling, outlier detection required). (3) Layer 3 diagnostic output assumes the enterprise accepts economic reasoning — if the enterprise rejects it ("we're different"), the entire three-layer chain loses validity. The GA plays the role of "theory translator" at this point.

Part VIII: Limitations & Preconditions

Preconditions for the Integration Matrix

(1) The "Overlap" classification assumes equivalent coverage depth between ME concepts and Synova's existing theory — in practice, ME's principal-agent theory emphasizes contract design while Grossman-Hart's property rights theory emphasizes ownership allocation; they are complementary rather than identical. THEORY.md should retain both perspectives. (2) "Gap" judgments are bounded by the current 14-paper literature scope — if new papers are added (e.g., Tirole's industrial organization, Camerer's behavioral game theory), some "gaps" may become "overlaps". (3) The Three-Layer Pyramid's Layer 1 assumes ME microeconomic principles are applicable across all industries — for heavily regulated industries (pharma, finance), institutional economics may be more suitable as the foundation layer than pure microeconomics.

Research Limitations

(1) The integration matrix is based on semantic matching of "theoretical concepts" rather than precise equivalence of "mathematical formulas" — this may lead to misclassification (two theories discussing the same concept using different variable names). (2) Policy implications are not compared — two theories may reach the same diagnosis but recommend different actions. (3) Thomas's ME framework is itself already a "fused product" — it has already made trade-offs between microeconomics, industrial organization, and behavioral economics. Applying a second fusion on top of this already-fused framework introduces "framework-on-framework" precision loss risk. (4) The integration proposals assume the existing expert/*/THEORY.md files have the structure and quality to absorb new content — this assumption was not verified in this route. (5) The "Human + AI Mixed Organization" frontier (F1 in Part V) is identified as a gap in both ME and existing theory — this is Synova's unique contribution opportunity but also the least academically grounded area, requiring original research rather than textbook integration.
